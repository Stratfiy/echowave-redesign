# Design: Voice as a step, and a human to catch it

Generated by /office-hours on 12 September 2026
Branch: `claude/eloquent-brown-l30svz`
Repo: Stratfiy/echowave-redesign
Status: DRAFT
Mode: Startup

---

## Problem statement

The stated goal: *"voice agents are just a part of business operations. I want to build a
platform where humans work alongside agents."*

The diagnostic changed that problem twice.

1. It started as a platform expansion, which was the wrong shape: a platform inherits the
   demand of its wedge, and the voice wedge has pilots but no customer who would scramble.
2. Then a real signal arrived: **clients who want voice agents inside n8n workflows.** That
   reframes it. Those clients already have a space where humans and agents work together.
   It is n8n. They are not asking for another one. They are asking for the voice step
   inside the one they have.

**The problem worth solving:** a business process that mixes automated steps and human
steps has nowhere good to put the human step when an agent cannot finish. The handoff is
where the value leaks, and no automation tool handles it well.

---

## Demand evidence

Honest ladder, strongest first.

| Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|
| Clients asking for voice agents inside n8n workflows | **Real.** Named channel, named integration point, buyers already paying the automation tax |
| Two pilots running (clinic, agri outreach), one 80,000-call engagement scoped | Intent, not yet demand. No payment, no dependency |
| Interest in a human-plus-agent workspace | **None.** Q1 answered "conversations and interest only" |

**The gap that matters:** nobody has been observed working. Q2 ("how does the operation run
today, badly") could not be answered. Everything below is designed to be correct under
uncertainty about that answer, and to produce the answer as a side effect.

---

## Status quo

For the n8n clients, the status quo is n8n itself: a visual workflow tool with hundreds of
connectors, orchestrating work that used to be manual. Voice is the step they cannot
automate, so today it stays a person with a phone.

For the pilot businesses, unknown. See The Assignment.

**The real competitor is not Vapi.** It is an ops executive or telecaller at roughly
₹15,000-35,000 a month who already works alongside whatever tools the business has, with
zero integration cost. That is the price ceiling and the comparison for anything sold as
"humans and agents working together."

---

## Target user and narrowest wedge

**The buyer that exists today:** the automation agency or technical consultant building n8n
workflows for Indian SMBs. They already have the client relationship, they already sell
outcomes, and they hit a wall the moment a process needs a phone call. `partners.py` and
`partner_admin.py` already model this buyer, with referrals and commission statements.

**The narrowest wedge:** one n8n node that places a Decibyl call as a workflow step and
returns a structured outcome, plus one screen where a failed step becomes a human task with
enough context to resolve it in under a minute.

Not a platform. Two components, four to six weeks, sold to people who already asked.

---

## Premises

Stated so they can be argued with. Four were put to the founder; none were contested.

1. **The platform inherits the wedge's demand.** No one is desperate today, so a wider
   product cannot be what creates desperation. Whatever is built next must be built inside
   a relationship that already exists.
2. **The human-in-the-loop primitives are already built and were never framed as product.**
   `organisation_memory` (agent extracts facts, human confirms or rejects at
   `POST /{fact_id}/status`), `services/review/`, `routes/evals.py`,
   `workflow_outcomes.py`, `missed_calls.py`, `notifications.py`, `team.py`, and
   transfer-to-human on all seven carriers.
3. **The unit of work is wrong for operations.** Everything is scoped to a *call*. Business
   work is scoped to a *job* spanning several calls, messages and human steps across days.
   Changing that abstraction is the platform bet, and it is not this quarter's bet.
4. **Voice is the thinnest-margin channel and the better one is already wired.**
   `constants.py:474` prices a WhatsApp message at ₹1.00 against ₹0.12 cost, 88% margin.
   `PRD.md` §4 puts voice at a fixed $0.020/min, 24% on the Indic stack and 7% on premium,
   and calls it "a pricing bug, not a pricing strategy."

Added after the n8n signal, and the most important one:

5. **The agent-workspace category is a capital fight Decibyl should not enter.** The
   reference class the founder named (Wingman, Buzz, Jio AI Hub, Hermes-style agent bots,
   Obsidian) converges on five properties: org memory, permissions, tasks, connectors, and
   both humans and agents as actors. Decibyl has four of the five. The missing one is a task
   queue where a human is an actor. But one of those competitors is Jio, and the
   differentiator Decibyl actually owns — Indian telephony licensing, KYC, TRAI and DLT, GST
   invoicing, prepaid reconciliation — is weak inside a workspace and strong underneath one.

## Landscape read

The five-property table, against what exists in this repository today:

| Property | Decibyl today | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Org memory | **Built** | `api/routes/organisation_memory.py`, `services/workflow/organisation_learning.py` |
| Permissions and roles | **Built** | `api/routes/team.py`, `organization_members.py`, `enums.py:691` role docs, staff tiers |
| Connectors | **Built** | Composio (its catalogue file: "Composio publishes 1,540 toolkits"), 14 MCP tools, 17 `sdk_expose` endpoints, Python and TypeScript SDKs |
| Agents as actors | **Built** | The workflow engine, node types, tool calling, branching |
| **Tasks with a human as actor** | **Missing** | No human work queue anywhere. `missed_calls` and `workflow_outcomes` are reports, not a queue |

One component away. That is the finding, and it is why the recommendation is not a rebuild.

## Approaches considered

### A. Escalation Inbox — minimal viable
One screen where every job an agent could not finish lands as a task with full context, a
human resolves it, and the agent resumes. Effort M, risk low. Reuses `missed_calls`,
`workflow_outcomes`, `notifications`, `team` roles, `call_transfer_manager`,
`organisation_memory`.

### B. The job graph — ideal architecture
Promote the workflow engine from call-scoped to job-scoped; agents and humans both become
assignable workers on a job's steps. Effort XL, risk high. Rewrites the abstraction all 374
endpoints and the whole billing model assume, while the wedge is still unvalidated.

### C. Agent supervisor, messaging-first — lateral
Sell the supervision rather than the labour: humans review, correct and approve what agents
did, WhatsApp-first. Effort M-L, risk medium. Opens a DLT and Meta template compliance
surface and partly cannibalises the voice story the marketing site sells.

### D. Voice as a step in the automation layer — added after the n8n signal
Ship an n8n node and lean on the existing MCP server so a call is a step in the client's
existing workflow. Decibyl owns the licensed, regulated half; the client's tool owns
orchestration. Effort S-M, risk low, and roughly 70% built already.

**Ruled out during the session:** B, because it commits a quarter to rewriting the core
abstraction before a single customer depends on the product. Revisit when the inbox has
usage data.

---

## Recommended approach

**D + A, shipped together as one story: "voice as a step, and a human to catch it."**

Not a compromise. They compose, and neither works as well alone:

- D without A means a voice step that fails silently inside someone else's automation, which
  is exactly the reliability complaint the roadmap's competitor research already identified
  as this category's top failure mode.
- A without D has no distribution, because nobody is asking for an inbox.

Together they are a coherent product sentence: *your automation can now make phone calls, and
when a call needs a person, a person gets it with everything they need to finish it.*

### Why this and not the workspace

- It is sold to buyers who already asked, so it converts interest into the first customer who
  would scramble if Decibyl vanished. That is the Q1 evidence the company does not have.
- It attacks where the moat is. Indian telephony is licensed, KYC-gated and TRAI-regulated.
  No workspace vendor in the reference class will cross that. `MANAGED_TELEPHONY.md` and the
  KYC flow are the defensible asset, not the canvas.
- It does not touch the call-to-job abstraction, so it is reversible.
- The platform is still reachable. Become the step every workflow depends on, then absorb the
  workspace, rather than launching a workspace against Jio with no demand evidence.

### Scope

**Build**
1. n8n community node: place call, wait for outcome, return structured result. Backed by the
   existing `sdk_expose` surface, not new endpoints.
2. Escalation Inbox: a task queue where a failed or flagged run becomes a human task carrying
   the transcript, what the agent tried, and what it needs. Resolve, and the run resumes.
3. Resume semantics on the workflow engine: a run that is paused awaiting a human, and the
   webhook back into n8n when it resolves.
4. Partner onboarding for agencies, using `partners.py` and `partner_admin.py` as they stand.

**Explicitly not building this quarter**
- The job graph (B). Not until the inbox has usage.
- A workspace, a chat surface, or a second canvas. n8n is the canvas.
- WhatsApp as a product (C). Keep it as a channel inside the inbox if a pilot asks.

## Open questions

1. **Are the n8n clients agencies or end businesses?** Changes the buyer, the price and
   whether `partners.py` is the front door. Ask them this week.
2. **Self-hosted or n8n Cloud?** Self-hosted n8n users skew technical and cheap, and may BYOK
   and route around the margin. `PRD.md` already concludes "BYOK customers are the ones who do
   not need us."
3. **What does a human actually do when a call fails?** Unknown until the field visit. The
   inbox's resolve action is a guess until then, and this design deliberately makes it the
   cheapest thing to change.
4. **Does the inbox belong in Decibyl or in n8n?** If the answer is n8n, D stands alone and A
   becomes a node, which is a smaller and possibly better business.
5. **Pricing.** Per call is the current model, per resolved task is the operations model, per
   seat is the workspace model. Do not decide until the inbox has usage.

## Success criteria

Four to six weeks from start, and each is a behaviour, not a feeling.

| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| n8n clients live on the node | at least 3, running real workflows, unattended |
| Someone would scramble | at least one client messages within an hour when a run fails |
| Inbox is used without prompting | a human opens and resolves tasks daily for two consecutive weeks |
| Resolution is fast | median time from task created to resolved under 5 minutes |
| Revenue | one paid invoice from an n8n client, GST-compliant, reconciled to the ledger |

If the first three are not true at six weeks, the human-alongside-agent thesis has not been
validated and the platform build should not start.

## Distribution plan

This is the half that is usually forgotten and here it is most of the value.

- **n8n community node** published to npm as `n8n-nodes-decibyl`, submitted to the n8n
  community list. This is the actual distribution channel, and it is a directory competitors
  are not in.
- **MCP server** already mounted at `/api/v1/mcp` with 14 tools. It is the same capability
  for any MCP-speaking agent, at zero additional build cost. Declare it publicly.
- **SDKs** already generated for Python and TypeScript from one spec, 17 exposed methods.
  Fix the OpenAPI security scheme first (audit finding F21): the published spec declares no
  authentication on any of 381 operations, so anything generated from it produces a client
  that 401s.
- **Partner channel** via `partners.py`, since agencies are the likely buyer.

---

## Dependencies and blockers

From the 12 September audit, these gate the plan rather than merely coexisting with it:

- **F21, OpenAPI declares no auth.** Blocks D directly. A node generated from that spec does
  not authenticate. Fix first, it is one decorator and a regenerate.
- **F39, 56% of responses have no schema**, including `test_phone_call`, the exact endpoint a
  voice node calls. Give the two `sdk_expose` routes a `response_model` before shipping a node
  against them.
- **F38, four error-body shapes.** An n8n node has to branch on failure. Add the exception
  handler.
- **F7, nothing polls the health endpoints.** Selling into someone else's automation means
  their run fails when you do. Wire the alert before taking dependency-grade customers.
- **F1 and F2, the Cloudonix findings.** Fix before onboarding agencies who will bring their
  own carrier configurations.
- **F5, embed text chat is ungated and unbilled.** Any human-agent surface must not repeat it:
  gate and meter the inbox from day one.

## What I noticed about how you think

- **You answered Q1 honestly when the flattering answer was available.** "Conversations and
  interest only" is the answer most founders dress up, and giving it straight is what made the
  rest of this session useful rather than decorative.
- **You surfaced the n8n clients only after I had already recommended something else**, which
  means the strongest fact you had was not the one you led with. That is worth watching: the
  real demand signal was sitting in your head while we planned around its absence.
- **Your instinct to look at the reference class was right and your conclusion from it was
  wrong.** Seeing Wingman, Buzz, Jio AI Hub and the rest converge on the same five properties
  is a good observation. Reading that as "I should build one too" skips the question of which
  of those five you could win on, and the answer is none of them — your win is the licensed,
  regulated layer underneath, which is exactly what none of them want to build.
- **You keep building the right primitives and then not naming them as product.** Org memory
  with human confirm-and-reject is a teaching loop. Outcome rate and readiness are a quality
  loop. Neither appears in `PRD.md` §5 differentiators. The gap in this company is not
  engineering, it is noticing what you have already shipped.

# Revision 2 — the vertical catalog, and what the evidence forced

Added 12 September 2026, same session. Two new inputs from the founder:

1. *"Along with workspace we also will have industry/business specific verticals with agents
   related to their business. Popular job roles from LinkedIn that our agents can do, and some
   task specific agents."*
2. Asked how many of the existing role templates have ever been sold or deployed:
   **"One or two, hand-configured by us."**

The second answer changed the plan more than the first.

## The catalog already exists

`api/services/agent_templates/catalogue.py` ships six role-shaped agents today: **Clinic front
desk, Property lead qualifier, Loan payment reminder, Admissions counsellor, COD order
confirmation, Restaurant reservations**, with `GET /agent-templates`, `GET /{id}` and
`POST /{id}/create`. The marketing site already sells eight verticals (clinics, dental,
ivf-fertility, diagnostics, real-estate, lending-collections, logistics, education).

The vertical agent catalog is not a thing to build. It is a thing that exists, has never been
surfaced as a front door, and has never been configured by anyone outside the founding team.

## The contradiction that had to be resolved

`ROADMAP.md`, 2 September, on the template gallery it calls the highest-ROI item on the page:

> Templates are vertical-agnostic. The same six work for a clinic, a salon, a gym, a coaching
> centre, a garage. **Strip the nouns and the job is identical:** a business pays for leads,
> the phone rings, nobody picks up, the money is gone.

The proposal in this session is the opposite conclusion, three weeks later, with no new
evidence in between. The September position is better supported, and by this company's own
measurements: when vertical-specific marketing content was written, the eight city pages came
out **60% identical to each other**, four of the five FAQs byte-for-byte the same, 61% of
visible words shared (measured in the 12 September audit). That is not a writing failure. It
is what happens when the underlying offer genuinely is one thing wearing eight names.

## Where the vertical actually lives

Not in the agent. In three cheap, composable things:

| What differs by vertical | Example | Where it lives | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Connector, the system of record | EMR vs LMS vs DMS vs LOS | Composio, 1,540 toolkits | Low |
| Guardrail, what it may say | RBI collections conduct, clinical no-advice | Prompt plus a refusal list | Low |
| Outcome schema, what success is | booking made vs payment promised vs document collected | `workflow_outcomes` | Low |
| **Call flow** | greet, understand, route, close | The workflow engine | **Identical. This is the expensive part and it does not change.** |

Six agents plus per-vertical connector, guardrail and outcome schema yields roughly forty
sellable products. Six agents times twenty rebuilt call flows yields twenty things nobody
maintains. With five people and `evals/` carrying no test coverage at all (audit finding), the
second number is fiction.

## On job roles as a product frame

- **Sell the job role, do not build the job role.** The name on the tin is how buyers search,
  and `/ai-receptionist/[city]` already does this. Keep the marketing frame.
- **A job title as a product promise anchors price to a salary and the bar to a whole job
  done.** The comparison becomes a ₹15,000-35,000/month human rather than a missed call. That
  bar is where most AI-SDR-shaped companies died.
- **LinkedIn titles are the wrong selection filter.** They select for what sounds impressive,
  not for what an agent can finish. The right filter is three tests: is there a clear success
  signal, is there a system of record to write to, and is the person on the other end expecting
  the call? Run the existing six through it.
- **"Task-specific agents" is the defensible half**, and it was said in the same sentence. A
  task is a step, a step composes into n8n, a job role does not.

## Two doors, one engine

| Door | Motion | Buyer | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Template gallery | Retail, rep-led, pick a role and fill six fields | SMB direct | Built in code, never surfaced, never self-served |
| n8n node plus MCP | Wholesale, agency-led, composable steps | Automation agencies | Not built; the only demand evidence the company has |

Both are packagings of one workflow engine, so both are cheap. Running two go-to-markets with
five people while adding twenty agents is not cheap, and is the thing to refuse.

## The decisive fact, and the experiment it forces

Templates have only ever been configured by the founders. The commission sales model
(`ROADMAP.md`: *"sales is commission-based, so headcount scales without burn, but only if a rep
can sell and set up with no training"*) rests entirely on a claim nobody has tested.

**The n8n clients are the handover test.** An agency configuring an agent for its own client is
structurally the same actor as an untrained rep, except it pays rather than costs. One motion
tests four unknowns:

| Unknown | How this motion answers it |
|---|---|
| Will anyone pay? | An invoice, GST-compliant, reconciled to the ledger |
| Can a non-founder configure an agent unaided? | The agency gets a client live with no founder in the room |
| Is the human-alongside-agent thesis real? | Do they open and resolve inbox tasks without being asked |
| Which verticals are real? | Which jobs they request that the existing six do not cover |

## Revised sequencing

**Now, 4-6 weeks, unchanged in substance from Revision 1 plus one addition**
1. n8n community node, backed by existing `sdk_expose` endpoints.
2. Escalation Inbox, the missing fifth property.
3. Resume semantics and the webhook back into n8n.
4. **Expose the six existing templates through the node and the API as starting points.** Not a
   gallery UI. Just make them reachable, so an agency can pick one instead of starting empty.

**Explicitly not now**
- New vertical agents. The six are unsold.
- The gallery UI. Build it once a non-founder has succeeded with the six; before that it is a
  storefront for untested inventory.
- The workspace. No demand evidence.
- The job graph. Not until the inbox has usage.

**Catalog growth rule, from here on**
> A new vertical agent gets built only after someone outside the founding team has sold or
> deployed that job twice from a generic template.

The catalog then grows out of revenue rather than ambition, and no agent is maintained that
nobody bought. Per-vertical connectors, guardrails and outcome schemas are exempt from this
rule, because they are cheap and composable and are where the vertical actually lives.

## Revised assignment

Replaces the Revision 1 assignment, which stands but is now second priority.

**Give one n8n client the node, the six templates and the inbox, and stay out of the room.**
Do not configure it for them. Do not join the call. Watch what they message you, and count how
many minutes pass before they need you. That number is the handover test, and it is the single
most important unmeasured quantity in the company.

The field visit stays on the list. It answers what the human in the loop actually does. This
answers whether anyone but you can run the product at all, and it gates the hiring model.

# Revision 3 — non-voice agents, cross-app triggers, and the line

Added 12 September 2026, same session. Two more inputs:

1. *"Even non-voice agents, like a content poster, where the user says every day from
   Drive take this file and post it on Meta."*
2. *"Instead of a webhook trigger it should be some action in an app logically
   triggering another app. Like every time a shipment fails, notify the customer. But
   across apps."* Plus: agents with KPIs.

Those two examples fall on opposite sides of the line that defines this company, and
naming the line is the whole of this revision.

## The line

| Test | Content poster | Shipment-failure notification |
|---|---|---|
| Needs judgment? | No | Yes |
| Can fail in a way a human must catch? | No | Yes |
| Outcome beyond an HTTP 200? | No | Yes |
| Anyone sets a KPI on it? | No | Yes |

**Four noes: it is an n8n node and someone else should run it. Four yeses: it is a
Decibyl agent.** Apply this before building anything.

The content poster is a four-node n8n workflow. Building it makes Decibyl a worse
Zapier. The shipment-failure notification is already voice- and WhatsApp-shaped, has a
conversation that can go wrong, and has a business outcome worth measuring.

## Why the trigger layer is the wrong thing to build

1. **It competes with the tool that produced the only demand evidence in this session.**
   The clients asking for voice agents chose n8n. An orchestration layer asks them to
   migrate off the thing that made them customers.
2. **We do not have it.** `api/services/integrations/composio/` is actions-only; there
   is no trigger, webhook or subscribe path in the client. Cross-app triggers are not a
   capability to surface, they are a system to build.
3. **The hard part is that the events mostly do not exist.** Most SaaS applications do
   not emit the event anyone actually wants. Courier "shipment failed" and bank "payment
   bounced" are polled, not pushed. So "an action in app A triggers app B" means polling
   infrastructure, change detection, deduplication, per-vendor rate limits and OAuth
   token refresh, across every connector, permanently.
4. **The moat in that category is connector count and maintenance, not logic.** Zapier
   is at roughly 8,000 integrations over two decades; Make is past a thousand; n8n has
   hundreds. Five people do not win a connector treadmill, and the treadmill is the
   entire product.

**Decision: rent orchestration and actions. n8n or the customer's own tool orchestrates.
Composio supplies actions (1,540 toolkits). Decibyl supplies the agent, the outcome and
the human backstop.**

## What is actually ours: the outcome layer

Zapier, n8n and Make are step executors. They report that a *run* succeeded. None of
them reports whether the *business goal* was met. That gap is unclaimed and we are
already half-built into it:

- `api/routes/workflow_outcomes.py` ships `/{workflow_id}/outcome-rate` and
  `/{workflow_id}/readiness`.
- `api/services/review/`, `api/routes/evals.py`, and `organisation_memory`'s
  confirm-and-reject loop are the correction half.
- `PRD.md` §8 already names the metric that closes deals: *"Cost per completed outcome.
  Spend ÷ successful dispositions. The number a customer actually cares about."*
- The 7 September audit raised this as finding #8 and it is still open:
  `api/services/reports/org_metrics.py:133` emits only `cost_per_call_paise`, with no
  successful-outcome denominator.

**Positioning that follows from it:**

> **Zapier runs steps. We run jobs that have to work.**

An agent with a job, a target, a human backstop, and a number that says whether the goal
was met.

## Non-voice is not new work, it is unfinished work

`api/services/messaging/` already contains `email.py`, `platform_whatsapp.py`,
`announce.py`, `follow_up.py` and `send.py`, and `api/services/workflow/dto.py` already
exposes a `channel` property with WhatsApp among its options. The channel abstraction
exists; it is not surfaced as a product.

Per `pricing-and-unit-economics.md`, WhatsApp runs at 88% gross margin against voice at
35-65%. **Non-voice is the higher-margin half of the business, not a dilution of it.**

## Effect on the plan

No change to the Revision 2 sequencing, and one addition to the build list.

**Added to the now-list**
5. **A KPI per agent.** One target, one measured rate, one cost-per-successful-outcome,
   visible to the customer. Close audit finding #8 by giving `cost_by_outcome` its second
   denominator. This is a small change to an existing report and it is the number that
   differentiates the product from every automation tool.

**Added to the not-now list**
- Any cross-app trigger, polling or event-detection infrastructure. Rent it.
- Any agent that passes the four-noes test. A content poster is an n8n workflow.
- A connector catalogue of our own. Composio is the catalogue.

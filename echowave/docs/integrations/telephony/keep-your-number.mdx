## Why forward instead of switching

A clinic with its number painted on the board, printed on prescription pads
and listed on Google Maps does not want a new number. Every one of those
places is a place the old number keeps ringing, and a business cannot change
them all at once.

Forwarding solves it in the other direction: the number everyone already has
stays yours, and the calls it cannot answer arrive at a Decibyl agent instead
of at nobody.

You keep:

- The number on your signage, your cards and your Maps listing
- Your existing SIM and plan
- The ability to answer in person whenever you want to

<Note>
Forwarding is set on **your** line, by you, from your own handset. Decibyl
cannot set it for you — no platform can, because the instruction goes to your
telecom operator from the SIM that owns the number.
</Note>

## Pick which calls the agent should take

This is the decision that matters, and most businesses get more out of the
second or third option than the first.

| What you want | Forward on |
|---|---|
| The agent answers everything | Always |
| Somebody answers when free, the agent catches the rest | Busy, and no reply |
| The agent covers nights and Sundays only | Always — switched on when you close |
| The agent catches calls when the phone is off or out of range | Unreachable |

**Busy + no reply is the common answer for a front desk.** The receptionist
keeps answering; the agent picks up the second and third caller who would
otherwise have heard an engaged tone, and the ones nobody reached in time.

## Set it from your handset

These are the standard GSM codes. Dial them like a phone number, from the SIM
whose calls you want forwarded, and replace `<decibyl number>` with the number
shown on your agent's Channels tab — including the country code, as
`+91XXXXXXXXXX`.

| Forward on | Turn on | Turn off |
|---|---|---|
| Always | `**21*<decibyl number>#` | `##21#` |
| Busy | `**67*<decibyl number>#` | `##67#` |
| No reply | `**61*<decibyl number>#` | `##61#` |
| Unreachable | `**62*<decibyl number>#` | `##62#` |
| *(all of the above)* | — | `##002#` |

To set busy **and** no reply, dial the two codes one after the other. They are
separate settings and do not replace each other.

### Check it took

Dial `*#21#` to see what your line is currently forwarding, and to where. Do
this every time — the confirmation a handset shows after dialling a code is
sometimes shown whether or not the operator accepted it.

<Note>
On some Indian networks the codes are handled by the operator's own app or
service number rather than by the handset, and on a **landline** they are
almost always set by the operator on request rather than by dialling. If
`*#21#` does not confirm the forward, call your operator and ask them to set
"call forwarding on busy and no answer" to the Decibyl number. It is a routine
request; the person on the other end will know it.
</Note>

## Then test it, before you rely on it

Forwarding that was set but not confirmed is the failure that hurts, because
it looks identical to working until a caller is lost.

1. Ring your own number from a different phone and let it ring out. The agent
   should answer.
2. If you forwarded on busy, be on a call and have somebody else ring you. The
   agent should answer.
3. Open the agent and check the call is listed with what it did.

If the call rings out to your own voicemail instead of the agent, the forward
did not take. Re-dial `*#21#` and check.

## What this costs

Two legs, and only one of them is ours:

- **Your operator** charges you for forwarding the call from your number to
  ours. This is a normal outgoing-call charge on your own plan, and it is
  between you and your operator.
- **Decibyl** charges for the call the agent answers, at your per-minute rate.

A forwarded call is therefore slightly more expensive than a call to a Decibyl
number directly. For most businesses that is worth paying to keep the number
their customers already know, but it is worth knowing before the first bill.

## When to use a Decibyl number instead

Forwarding is the right answer when the number matters. Take a
[managed number](/integrations/telephony/managed-numbers) instead when:

- You are starting a new line anyway — a campaign number, a new branch
- You want calls placed *out*, not just answered; a forwarded number cannot
  make outbound calls
- Your line is a landline your operator will not forward

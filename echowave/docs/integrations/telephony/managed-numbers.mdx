## Overview

A managed number is a phone number Decibyl rents from the carrier on your
behalf. You get a number without opening a carrier account, and we handle the
telecom compliance application, the number-to-application wiring, and the
monthly rental.

The alternative is bring-your-own: you open a Plivo (or Twilio, Telnyx,
Vonage…) account yourself and add its credentials under
[Telephony Configurations](/integrations/telephony/overview). That path is
unchanged and needs none of what follows — your numbers sit under your own
carrier account, verified in your own name.

<Note>
Managed numbers are India-only today. Indian numbers require the telecom
licensee to verify each end customer, which is why the verification step below
exists and cannot be skipped.
</Note>

## What it costs

| | |
|---|---|
| Monthly rental | ₹499 per number |
| Billed | Monthly by autopay, from the bank account you authorise |
| First month | Prorated by day from the day you buy |

The rental accrues whether or not you place a single call. It is a separate
line from per-call charges — a quiet month still costs the rental.

If autopay is ever revoked, the rental falls back to your prepaid credit
balance and the unpaid schedule below applies. Nothing is released because you
turned autopay off.

## Before you can buy a number

Your account has to be verified by the telecom licensee. This is a real
regulatory requirement under the DoT directive of June 2025, not a Decibyl
policy, and no amount of configuration works around it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enter your business details">
    Go to **Verification** and enter your legal entity name, GSTIN and
    registered address.

    <Warning>
    The business name must match your registration certificate **character for
    character**, including suffixes like "Pvt Ltd" and any punctuation. The
    carrier compares them exactly. A name that differs by a suffix is the
    single most common cause of rejection, and the rejection message will not
    tell you that is what happened.
    </Warning>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Upload your documents">
    For a company:

    - **Registration certificate** — a Certificate of Incorporation **or** a
      Udyam (MSME) registration. Either is accepted; many small businesses have
      only the latter.
    - **GST certificate**

    For an individual: an authorised signatory ID and an address proof.

    <Warning>
    Upload a **different file to each slot**. A single PDF containing several
    certificates looks complete on screen and is rejected by the carrier as a
    missing document. We block this before submission, but it is worth knowing
    why.
    </Warning>

    Files must be PDF, JPEG or PNG, and under 5MB.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Submit for verification">
    We check the documents are complete and legible, then forward them to the
    carrier. **Our approval does not enable calling** — it is a pre-screen so
    that a carrier round trip is not wasted on a blurry scan.

    The carrier's decision is the one that opens telephony. It usually takes a
    few working days.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Verification states

| Status | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| `not_started` | Nothing uploaded yet | Upload documents |
| `submitted` / `under_review` | With our team | Wait. Keep building and testing over the browser meanwhile |
| `rejected` | We sent it back | Read the reason, fix, resubmit |
| `forwarded` | With the telecom operator | Wait |
| `carrier_approved` | **Verified.** You can buy numbers and place calls | — |
| `carrier_rejected` | The operator declined | Read the reason, fix, resubmit |
| `suspended` | The operator paused a previously approved account | Read the reason. Your numbers are still yours; most suspensions lift once the document in question is updated |
| `expired` | The application lapsed | Upload current documents and submit again |

While you are unverified you can still build agents and test them over the
browser — WebRTC involves no phone number and no licensee.

## Buying a number

Search what the carrier has available:

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.decibyl.ai/api/v1/managed-numbers/search \
  -H "X-API-Key: $DECIBYL_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "telephony_configuration_id": 1,
    "country_iso": "IN",
    "number_type": "local",
    "city": "Hyderabad"
  }'
```

An empty list is a normal answer. Indian local inventory is thin and moves
during the day — try a different city, or try again later.

### Authorise autopay first

A number is monthly rent from the day it is issued, so a standing instruction
has to be in place before one is handed over. Start it once per account:

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.decibyl.ai/api/v1/billing/mandate \
  -H "X-API-Key: $DECIBYL_API_KEY"
```

The response carries an `authorisation_url` — a Razorpay-hosted page where the
authorisation actually happens (UPI Autopay, card, or eNACH). Open it in a
browser and complete it there.

<Note>
Completing that page does not immediately make the mandate usable. The
authorisation is confirmed by your bank, and Decibyl only treats it as valid
once the payment provider tells us so. Poll `GET /billing/mandate` until
`authorised` is `true` — usually seconds, occasionally longer.
</Note>

Buying before the mandate is authorised is refused with **403** and a message
saying so. That is deliberate: a number issued against an unauthorised mandate
is rent nobody has agreed to pay.

### Then buy one

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.decibyl.ai/api/v1/managed-numbers \
  -H "X-API-Key: $DECIBYL_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "telephony_configuration_id": 1,
    "address": "+918041234567",
    "label": "Support line",
    "inbound_workflow_id": 42
  }'
```

Buying does three things at once: rents the number, binds it to the
application that routes inbound calls to your agent, and links your compliance
application to it. If any step fails, the number is released back to the
carrier — you are never left paying for a number that does not work.

Set `inbound_workflow_id` to the agent that should answer. You can attach one
later, but a number with no agent rejects inbound calls.

## When a rental goes unpaid

With autopay authorised, the rental is collected from your bank on the monthly
cycle and never touches your prepaid credit. If the mandate is revoked, halted
by your bank, or was never set up, collection falls back to that balance —
which is where the schedule below starts. If the balance is
short, here is exactly what happens — no step is skipped and nothing happens
sooner than stated:

| Day | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0 | The charge fails. We retry daily. **Calls still work** |
| 7 | Calls on that number are paused. **The number is still yours** |
| 15, 25 | Reminders |
| 45 | The number becomes *eligible* for release |

You are emailed before any of this bites, not after: a warning goes out when
your balance drops below about ten days of your recent usage, and a more urgent
one under three. The address used is every account member's, so keep at least
one that somebody reads.

Two things worth being clear about:

- **Topping up restores everything immediately.** A suspended number is fully
  recoverable — the number is still held at the carrier, and the next
  successful charge reactivates it and resets the clock.
- **Nothing is released automatically.** Day 45 is when release becomes
  permitted, not when it happens. Releasing a number is irreversible — it goes
  back to the carrier's pool and may be issued to someone else — so it always
  takes a deliberate action.

## Giving a number up

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.decibyl.ai/api/v1/managed-numbers/{id}/release \
  -H "X-API-Key: $DECIBYL_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"reason": "no longer needed", "force": true}'
```

<Warning>
Release is permanent. The number returns to the carrier's pool and can be
issued to someone else the same day. If it is printed on your signage,
vehicles or invoices, you will not get it back. A reason is required.
</Warning>

Billing stops when the number is released. We keep the record that you held it.

## Checking status

Ask Claude, if you have the [MCP server](/integrations/mcp) connected:

> "Why can't my agent make calls?"
> "What phone numbers do I have and what's answering on each?"
> "What am I paying monthly?"

Or over the API: `GET /api/v1/kyc` for verification, and the telephony
configuration endpoints for numbers.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The carrier rejected my application">
    Read `carrier_rejection_reason` on the verification page. The two most
    common causes are a business name that does not match the registration
    certificate exactly, and the same file uploaded to two document slots. Fix
    and submit again — we amend the existing application rather than filing a
    second one.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Search returns no numbers">
    Normal. Indian local inventory is thin. Try another city, or try again
    later. Retrying in a tight loop will not help and may hit the carrier's
    rate limit.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Inbound calls to my number are rejected">
    Either no agent is attached (`inbound_workflow_id` is null) or the number
    is suspended for non-payment. Check the number's `status` — `active` means
    routing is fine, `suspended` means the rental is unpaid.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="My account was approved and now says suspended">
    The licensee paused it, usually because a document lapsed. Your numbers are
    still held and calls resume when it is reinstated. The reason is on the
    verification page.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
